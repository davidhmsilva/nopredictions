No predictions. Just edges.

An AI trading agent that hunts for mispricings in football prediction markets. It doesn't try to forecast who wins — it builds its own fair-value estimate, compares it against the sharpest markets in the world, and acts only when the two meaningfully disagree. Every position is logged publicly, before the event resolves.

Venue: Polymarket
Benchmark: Betfair · Pinnacle
Sport: Football
Mode: Public & transparent

The Idea

Two waves, at the same time

The whole project sits at the intersection of two things happening right now.

①

AI can do real analysis

Research that used to take a desk of human analysts can now be done by a machine, continuously, without fatigue or bias.

②

Prediction markets are hot

Polymarket and similar venues are having their cultural moment — huge attention and volume, but comparatively thin systematic "sharp" money keeping prices honest.

The thesis: where a lot of attention meets relatively little disciplined pricing, mistakes appear. A patient, math-driven agent can find them. It never claims to know the future — it only points out when a market's price looks wrong relative to a better estimate.

The Engine

From raw data to a fair price

Three layers feed into every decision. The middle layer — the agent's own estimate of fair value — is the heart of the system.

Build, don't copy

The agent forms its own opinion of fair value first. It isn't just mirroring a bookmaker's price.

Sharps = truth oracle

The world's most efficient markets are used as a reality check: if our line is far from theirs, we distrust our line, not the market.

Polymarket = where we act

Once we trust our number, the question becomes: does Polymarket's price disagree enough to be worth a position?

The Signal

What counts as an “edge”

An edge is simply a gap: the agent thinks an outcome is more likely than the market's price implies. Below, a worked example with illustrative numbers.

If the agent believes the true chance is well above what the market charges, buying that outcome is a positive-expectation bet — even if this particular match still loses. Edges only pay off over many, many positions.

But a raw gap isn't enough

Most apparent edges are mirages — model error, stale prices, or markets that already know something we don't. Before acting, the agent puts every candidate through filters:

  • Does the sharp-market consensus agree the price is off?
  • Is the gap big enough to survive our own uncertainty?
  • Can the position actually be filled at a price that keeps the edge?
  • Are we already exposed to the same outcome elsewhere?

The exact gates and cut-offs are part of the secret sauce and are deliberately omitted.

The Loop

What the agent does, all day

It runs on a continuous cycle — pre-match and live, while games are in play. No human picks the bets; the math decides.

👁️ Observe

Continuously watch live market prices and match situations — not just a once-a-day snapshot.

🧮 Price

Re-estimate the fair probability for each outcome as new information arrives.

⚖️ Compare

Check our number against sharp books and against Polymarket's current price.

🎯 Act

If a filtered edge survives, take a small, fixed-size position — sometimes only on paper, sometimes live.

📊 Measure

Record the result and grade the decision against the closing line (see next page).

Scoring & Transparency

How we know if it's working

Winning money on a few bets proves nothing — that can be luck. The agent is judged on a stricter, professional standard.

📈

Closing Line Value (CLV)

The gold standard. Did we get a better price than the market's final price right before kickoff? Consistently beating the closing line is the real signature of edge — independent of whether any single bet won or lost.

🧪

Honest sample sizes

No conclusions from a handful of results. Performance is tracked over hundreds of positions, with yield and statistical significance — not cherry-picked highlights.

Luck vs. edge: positive returns with poor CLV is just luck and will fade. Modest returns with consistently positive CLV is a genuine, repeatable edge. We always report both.

Everything in the open

Public log

Every position is posted before the event resolves — including the losers and the bad calls.

Live dashboard
